# ChatGPT Deep Research Prompts

Use these prompts to gather evidence for calibrating the cumulative government simulator. Each prompt should ask for cited sources, comparative cases, usable model variables, plausible parameter ranges, and clear uncertainty notes.

## 1. Adaptive Capture and Lobby Venue Shifting

Research how organized interests adapt when lobbying disclosure, spending limits, public financing, participatory rulemaking, or anti-capture enforcement are introduced. Focus on venue shifting across legislatures, agencies, courts, procurement, ballot campaigns, think tanks, trade associations, and litigation finance. Compare the United States with at least five other democracies. Identify empirical estimates or case evidence that could calibrate a simulator variable for lobby adaptation pressure, capture-control decay, detection probability, sanction deterrence, and public-alignment erosion. Separate strong empirical findings from plausible but weakly evidenced mechanisms.

## 2. Emergency Powers, Rights Review, and Court-Curbing

Research historical cases where emergency powers, emergency litigation, or crisis governance changed the balance among legislatures, executives, courts, agencies, and voters. Include cases where judicial review restrained abuse and cases where courts were bypassed, packed, curbed, ignored, or overloaded. Identify conditions that predict rights-threatening policy inflow, review backlog, court-curbing pressure, compliance decay, and legitimacy loss. Produce simulator-ready variables and parameter ranges for emergency-abuse stress and court-curbing/noncompliance stress.

## 3. Reform Transition Costs and Sequencing

Research constitutional and institutional reform sequences: electoral-system changes, legislative procedure reforms, judicial-review redesigns, anti-corruption reforms, lobbying reforms, campaign-finance reforms, and agency-capacity reforms. Which reforms tend to work as prerequisites for others? Which fail when introduced too quickly or without administrative capacity? Identify evidence for transition load, sequencing benefits, implementation lag, political backlash, legal uncertainty, and institutional learning. Provide examples of phased reform paths that could be modeled as multi-period portfolios rather than single static designs.

## 4. Federalism, Agency Capacity, and Implementation Failure

Research how federalism, local government resistance, procurement constraints, civil-service capacity, judicial review, and administrative complexity shape implementation success or failure. Compare high-capacity and low-capacity democracies, and include examples where formally well-designed laws underperformed because agencies could not implement them. Identify simulator variables for agency implementation gap, federalism resistance, compliance loss, delivery bottleneck, review capacity, and public trust effects. Provide plausible parameter ranges and stress-test cases.

## 5. Public Feedback, Trust, Compliance, and Legitimacy

Research feedback loops between policy performance, public trust, democratic legitimacy, institutional compliance, turnout, polarization, protest, and support for court or agency independence. Identify when public dissatisfaction improves correction through elections or participation versus when it produces backlash, noncompliance, delegitimation, or authoritarian demand. Produce simulator variables for voter feedback pressure, legitimacy erosion, compliance decay, public-alignment correction, and trust recovery. Separate evidence about short-run crises from evidence about long-run institutional performance.

## 6. Citizen Agenda-Setting Mechanisms

Research citizen initiatives, referendums, deliberative assemblies, participatory budgeting, agenda petitions, citizens' assemblies, sortition panels, democracy vouchers, public comment systems, and participatory rulemaking. Which mechanisms improve public alignment without increasing capture, misinformation, administrative overload, or rights threats? Identify evidence for citizen agenda capacity, correction speed, deliberation quality, capture vulnerability, turnout/participation bias, agenda overload, and legitimacy effects. Recommend simulator-ready mechanisms not currently represented in the cumulative portfolio rows.

## 7. Calibration Review of Adaptive Bridge v1

Act as a skeptical institutional-design model auditor. Review the attached or described Adaptive Bridge v1 assumptions: transition load, party adaptation, court adaptation, lobby adaptation, agency implementation gap, voter feedback pressure, citizen agenda capacity, stress-profile modifiers, score weights, and recommendation-gate thresholds. Identify which assumptions are most weakly evidenced, which are directionally plausible, which could reverse rankings, and which should be replaced with empirical estimates. Return a prioritized calibration plan, suggested historical datasets, and a revised model-validation strategy.

## 8. Comparative Portfolio Families

Compare these institutional families as possible reform bundles: portfolio-hybrid legislatures, pairwise-alternatives legislatures, emergency-restraint judicial review, pre-enactment constitutional councils, democracy vouchers, budgeted disclosed lobbying, full anti-capture bundles, and citizen agenda mechanisms. For each family, identify best-case arguments, predictable failure modes, implementation prerequisites, likely opposition coalitions, court and agency interactions, and public feedback risks. Produce a table that separates empirical evidence, synthetic model implications, and speculative design recommendations.

Research whether pairwise agenda-setting, amendment tournaments, Condorcet-style legislative procedures, sequential pairwise voting, or structured alternative comparison have been used in legislatures, parties, councils, courts, deliberative assemblies, participatory budgeting, or public-choice experiments. Identify empirical evidence on agenda manipulation, cycling, strategic nomination, party control, voter/citizen comprehension, administrative load, minority-rights effects, and policy quality. Produce simulator-ready parameters for party adaptation pressure, agenda manipulation risk, public alignment, delivery speed, rights-risk inflow, legitimacy, and implementation complexity. Clearly separate real-world evidence from theory or laboratory results.

Identify where such rules improve delivery and where they worsen rights threats, capture, legitimacy, or noncompliance.
